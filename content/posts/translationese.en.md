Recently I’ve been tinkering with GitHub Pages and set up a personal blog with
both Chinese and English versions. I write each post in Chinese first, then let
AI translate it into English with a single click. Whenever I proofread the
translation, something about it always feels slightly off, though I never really
stopped to think about why.

Today I wrote another post, and the strange feeling of the AI
translation hit me even stronger. I started comparing the Chinese and English
versions of several articles side by side. Suddenly I felt a chill run down my
spine.

It reminded me of an old line from classical poetry:
_Once you enter the noble's gate, it's as deep as th sea;_
_from then on, the former love is but a stranger._

After graduating, every company I worked for was a foreign one. Documentation,
code, comments, emails—almost all of our written communication at work was in
English. Over the years, I’ve barely written anything serious in Chinese.

Looking back at the Chinese blog posts I had written recently, compared with the
AI’sliteral English translations, the sentences lined up almost one by one. The
so-called “translation” barely needed to change the structure at all.

That was when it finally dawned on me: I wasn’t really writing in Chinese. I
was simply following English patterns of thought and sentence structure, only
replacing the words with Chinese characters.

My native language—the one I thought I knew best—had quietly been replaced
over the years of daily work. Now that I’m trying to pick it up again, I
realize it no longer comes naturally.

Still, I hope that starting with this short piece, I might slowly rediscover
expressions that truly belong to Chinese.
